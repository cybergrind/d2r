How to price a D2R item — the method

Written 2026-09-18 for a Softcore / Non-Ladder / PC / Reign of the Warlock player farming Pindleskin, Hell cows and the Anya shop. This page is the procedure: how to go from a tooltip on the ground to “vendor”, “sell for X” or “keep”. The numbers it leans on live in the pricing primer (what is worth what, Traderie asks and diablo2.io fills pulled 2026-09-18); the pickup rules in the farm guide §3; the build's own needs in the Warlock page.

TL;DR — the five questions, in order.
  1. Who pays for this? Value comes from two places only: a runeword recipe that accepts the base, or an S/A-tier build that names the item or affix. If neither exists, stop: vendor.
  2. Does it pass the gate? Every priced item has one or two binary gates: exact socket count, ≥15 % Enhanced Defense/Damage for “Superior”, 45 all-res on a Paladin shield, +2 class skills on a circlet/amulet, 5/5 on a facet, +life on a skiller, 3 sockets on a Void dagger. Fail the gate = floor or vendor, whatever the rest says.
  3. How good is the roll? Past the gate, the roll multiplies price 3–10×: 15 ED vs plain, 45@ vs 40@, +45 life vs +30, 20 FCR vs 10.
  4. What do people ask, and what does it fill for? Read Traderie asks one vote per seller, take the min → median of the matching bucket; expect common rolls to fill near the median and chase rolls at 32–63 % of the ask.
  5. Sell, keep or vendor? Sell ≥ 0.8 Ist (a Mal) singly, 0.2–0.8 only in bulk, below 0.2 vendor; keep currency and anything your build wears.

Three rules that fall out of the card. 1 · Never cube runes upward from Pul on. 1 · Step 0 — what the question really is

“Is this worth anything?” means “is there a buyer with runes for exactly this item?”. D2R has no auction house; price is what a specific buyer pays for a specific need. Three facts follow, and they explain almost every verdict on this page:

  • Runewords set base demand. A white Archon Plate is worth something because Enigma / CoH / Fortitude want a 4-socket elite light armor. A white Gothic Plate is worth nothing because no S/A runeword names it. The runeword also fixes the exact socket count — a 3os Monarch is dead, a 4os is Spirit.
  • Builds set affix demand. A Warlock caster amulet sells because three Warlock guides are S tier this season; a +3 Javelin skills white javelin does not because Lightning Fury wants +2 Amazon on top. Demand shifts with the tier list: primer appendix B lists the 26 builds and their key items.
  • Runes are money. Everything is quoted in runes; this guide converts to Ist = 1 (§4). A rune never rolls badly, so it never loses value — which is why the currency runes (Pul and up) are always “keep”.

2 · Step 1 — triage by colour (item on the ground or in the tooltip)

The colour tells you which branch to read. Each branch ends in a verdict or in a pointer to the primer section with the numbers.

Yellow (rare)

Pattern before roll: the must-affixes and the required number of different supporting stats must be present. A supporting life, resistance or attribute roll below the table’s target is CHECK with the shortfall shown, not an automatic vendor. Missing stats, zero rolls, wrong bases and failed skill/speed/socket gates still fail. CHECK does not assign the higher-roll price.

A rare is a lottery of 4–6 affixes; it is worth something only when the slot's must-affixes all landed and one wrong roll did not eat a slot. Check three things before reading affixes:

  • Durability 3× the base? A “failed unique” (the unique was already found this game, or cannot spawn here) — it is an ordinary rare with extra durability, nothing more.
  • Base tier. Rare generic armor of normal or exceptional tier has no market, and neither has a rare weapon below the enhanced-damage gate in the next point (a rare Razor Bow with 138 ED / 20 IAS / 2os — vendor). Rare weapons at or above that gate are listed on every tier, because a rare can be upgraded in the cube; the upgrade cost is yours to review. Rare elite armor of generic type sells only with two or more good rolls on a runeword-grade base (Archon, Monarch…).
  • Rare physical weapons. Enhanced damage is the gate. Ethereal swords, axes, maces, hammers, clubs, scepters, claws, spears, polearms, javelins and throwing weapons with at least 200% enhanced damage warrant CHECK; so do non-ethereal bows and Amazon bows at 200%, crossbows at 300%, and non-ethereal Amazon javelins and elite-type claws at 200%. Attack speed, both Fool’s level-scaling stats, self-repair, indestructibility, a socket and an elite base raise the ask but are not required. A non-ethereal Phase Blade (intrinsically indestructible) still needs 300% and 30 IAS, or 200% with 30 IAS and both Fool’s stats. No numerical price is inferred from the pattern.
  • Echoing buff-switch weapons. Compare +3 Warcries throwing weapons with the same base, ethereal status and other modifiers. A missing IAS suffix is zero IAS for this comparison; use copies with equal or lower IAS, including plain Echoing weapons. Do not mix strength, charges or other suffixes. This prices the Warcries switch role, not physical throwing damage.
  • Rune and gem sale lots. An exact supported lot is assessed against the 0.25-Ist threshold using its total asking value. Keep the source quote per unit and display both, for example 20 Perfect Rubies: 1 Ist per lot (0.05 each). A single drop or smaller stack shows CHECK to save toward a supported sale-sized lot, with the target quantity and dated lot ask. It is not priced as an immediately sellable single item. Mixed rune sets and Random Gems are not interchangeable single-type lots.
  • Rare skill-glove comparisons. For +2 skill-tree/20-IAS/3-mana-leech gloves, cached price comparisons may vary lightning resistance downwards while holding the base, ethereal status and every other modifier fixed. Three sellers are required for a sale estimate; smaller samples remain CHECK. The local affix table limits glove lightning resistance to 5–30. Better resistance rolls never set a weaker copy’s price.
  • Magic skill gloves. +3 Bow, Javelin or Martial Arts skills with 20 IAS use scoped cached price bands only for the matching skill, base, ethereal status and full modifier set. Other variants remain CHECK. The 2026-10-03 cache has three supported Gauntlets cohorts (at least three independent priced sellers each); a broad glove ask is reference only.
  • Physical throwing weapons. Ethereal rare throwing axes, knives and javelins follow the 200% enhanced-damage gate above; 30 IAS is what Double Throw wants but sellers do not require it. Normal and exceptional bases require an upgrade-cost review. Throwing Mastery replenishes quantity on critical strikes, so a replenish affix is not required. No numerical price follows from the pattern.
  • Physical Assassin claws. Rare claws follow the same gate: ethereal with at least 200% enhanced damage, or non-ethereal elite-type claws at 200%. The older full combination (both Fool’s scaling stats, 200% ED and 30 IAS) remains a CHECK pattern for the other non-ethereal claws. Lower-tier bases require an upgrade-cost review, and ethereal copies without repair or indestructibility a repair/Zod review; no ready-to-use claim or numerical price is inferred. Caster skill rules are separate.
  • Slot. Rings, amulets, gloves, boots, belts, circlets and class items are the slots where rares compete with uniques — read them against the table.

4.3 Asks are not fills

  • One vote per seller. A bucket of 47 listings from 6 sellers is a wall, not a market. Traderie prices[].group entries are OR-alternatives — take the cheapest.
