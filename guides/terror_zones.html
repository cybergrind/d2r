Terror Zones — Herald progression and farming

Softcore · Non-Ladder · PC · RotW · Patch 3.2 article audited 2026-09-21. Research status: ordered model-ranked trial routes available; hourly optimum unverified.

Start with the ordered queues in §4. Each act and ALL now has an explicit start, complete fallback order and stay/leave rule. These are simulator-selected trial routes for Tier 5 spawns per ordinary kill, including progression. The goal remains Tier 5 Herald kills per hour; missing travel/combat timings and runtime semantics prevent claiming these queues are optimal for that goal. After Tiers 1–4 move on in A1/A2/A3/A5/ALL; in A4 leave only after Tiers 1–3. At Tier 5, finish the remaining groups.

Preconditions & working assumptions
  • Every recommendation requires evidence. Article-derived facts below are attributed; modeled numbers are not observed game rates.
  • The requested scenarios are each act terrorized alone, then all acts terrorized together. The current manual-terrorization configuration must confirm zone membership and exclusions for these scenarios.
  • Target metric is completed Tier 5 kills per total elapsed farming time. Include initial tiers, travel, loading, combat, looting policy, failed spawns, resets and new-game overhead. Spawning Tier 4, unlocking Tier 5, spawning Tier 5, and killing Tier 5 are separate milestones.
  • Player count, actual Echoing Strike gear, mobility, session length and terrorization duration remain measurement inputs; no invented seconds-per-kill defaults. The route comparator scores spawns per ordinary kill, assumes every Herald is immediately killed at zero cost, treats all article groups as eligible, and ignores transit kills. It compares a full route without revisits or time limits.
  • The diagnostic assumes all monsters already populated, equal monster weights, fixed original population denominator, zero base chance, pre-kill completion and immediate successful spawn/tier advancement. It resets only the local kill counter, leaves dead monsters dead, and excludes Herald/minion kills. Continuous completion is the default; integer flooring is an explicit alternative. These are model assumptions pending verification.
Rejected ideas (and why)
  • Calling a size-only order an hourly optimum: size alone measures neither travel time nor progression failure. Ascending and descending populations are permitted candidate queues; the comparator evaluates their full stochastic progression instead of declaring either inherently best.
  • “Always leave after a Herald”: can abandon useful Tier 5 opportunities after Tier 4, and queued Heralds may require another room entry in the same level.
  • Replacing each random spawn with the expected kill number: loses no-spawn outcomes and the distribution of remaining monsters.
  • Treating a flattened multi-level population as a faithful runtime model or verified farming recommendation: it ignores per-level weights and route-dependent room activation. The disclosed exploratory comparator may use this approximation, but cannot validate an hourly-optimal route.
  • Publishing an optimal order from assumed travel times or the old public token-based configuration: unsupported.

1 · What the available evidence says

The article describes one spawn roll per eligible monster kill in a terrorized area. A zone_data_id groups levels sharing completion; it is not necessarily one map, one displayed Terror Zone, or one act. On a Herald spawn, completion resets. Tier-specific slopes change the chance curve. The documentation also describes an added herald_base_chance_to_spawn; its effective Patch 3.2 value and any overrides must be extracted.

The chance is zero until the tier’s threshold, then rises along the logistic curve; Tier 5 starts earliest (5.7% completion) but caps near 2.4% per kill, so a Tier 5 spawn is likely over a full clear yet rarely early.

Examples from the article: Blood Moor and Den of Evil have separate counters. Barracks is separate from the shared three Jail levels. Far Oasis and each Maggot Lair level are independent. Inner Cloister, Cathedral and Catacombs share one counter with weights 0.5, 1, 2, 2, 2, 1; fully clearing Catacombs 2–4 contributes 5/8.5 of that group in the article’s fully populated approximation.

A successful roll is not necessarily an immediately visible Herald. The field documentation says a failed monster placement is queued and retried on entry to another room in the same level. The optimizer must account for collection of that Herald before recommending departure.

2 · What the executable diagnostic establishes

The tool rolls spawn/no-spawn once per kill, advances tiers only on success, resets the modeled local progress, and consumes a finite population. It also computes the exact first-spawn probability to check the random simulation. It does not emulate weighted rooms, travel or combat. The separate route comparator in §4 uses this same approximation to compare explicit queues.

3 · When should we leave after a Herald?

For the simplified model, let N be the original population and R the monsters left immediately after the reset. Compute each remaining kill’s next-tier hazard using kills since reset, not kills already spent. If every remaining roll has zero hazard, there is no progression benefit to staying. The cow example above has this property.

The next-tier threshold falls sharply after Tier 4, so leftovers that cannot produce Tier 2 may still produce Tier 5. “Skip below Tier 5” is therefore too broad. When the remaining chance is positive, compare expected Tier 5 reward and time for staying versus moving, including access cost, failed-zone fallback, and the value of preserving untouched monsters for later tiers. If revisits preserve progress, partial clears become possible actions too.

This must be an adaptive policy keyed by actual tier, progress and monsters remaining. A fixed order alone cannot cover early spawns, late spawns, no spawn, delayed placement and revisits.

4 · Ordered routes to try

These are model-ranked trial routes, not verified Heralds/hour optima. The comparison uses the article’s mean populations and tier slopes, flattens multi-level weights, and has no travel or combat times. Its score is Tier 5 spawns per 1,000 ordinary kills over a complete route, including unsuccessful progression. A slower trip can erase a small modeled advantage. In particular, the cross-act route assumes no cost for switching acts.

How to follow every queue

  1. Start a fresh game with only the listed act terrorized, or all acts for ALL. Begin at step 1. The model assumes progression starts with the next Herald at Tier 1.
  2. Clear the current listed Herald group until its Herald appears or the group runs out of monsters. If it produces no Herald, move to the next step without advancing your tier. A grouped entry means all its named levels contribute; clearing just one floor is not a complete attempt.
  3. Kill the Herald before moving. Resolve a queued spawn by entering another room in that same level if needed. Track actual successful Heralds, not visited zones: the fourth zone does not necessarily produce Tier 4.
  4. Acts I, II, III, V and ALL: after each Tier 1, 2, 3 or 4 Herald, abandon the remaining monsters in that group and move to the next listed step. Act IV: leave after Tiers 1–3, but after Tier 4 stay and finish the current group to seek Tier 5, then continue.
  5. Once Tier 5 is available: fully clear each remaining listed group, killing every Herald that appears. Continue after a Tier 5 Herald; do not apply the progression departure rule to it. Do not return to groups abandoned earlier in this baseline.
  6. At the end of the queue, end the modeled run. If repeating, start a fresh game and restart from step 1. This is a reproducible full-route test, not a proven best remake point. Record total elapsed time and Tier 5 kills to compare actual hourly yield.

Numbers in parentheses are article-average ordinary monsters, transcribed 2026-09-21; they are not kill targets. Follow the actual Herald event. These queues specify where to fight, not navigation through prerequisites. They assume reaching each entry without killing monsters in later entries. If access requires fighting through another group, that changes its state and this simulation no longer predicts the run. Tomb labels refer to the article’s size categories, not fixed symbols; identifying those categories on an unseen map is an unresolved practical limitation.

Why the large starting zones in ALL? The requested goal includes farming after progression, not merely reaching Tier 5 quickly. With no travel cost and constant ordinary-kill cost, the model can favor consuming large groups during progression and saving small groups for Tier 5. Actual seconds per group can reverse that result. Treat this as a controlled route to measure, not a general claim that Worldstone is the fastest start.
